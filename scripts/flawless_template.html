1. Introduction

This project implements an end-to-end analytical framework for retail supermarket transactions using Data Warehouse, Online Analytical Processing (OLAP), and Data Mining techniques in Weka. Modern retail stores capture thousands of transaction records daily at point-of-sale (POS) cash registers. Transforming these operational logs into structured dimensional models enables retail store managers to optimize inventory procurement, discover product affinities, analyze customer demographic preferences, and boost overall retail margins.

2. Problem Statement

Supermarket transactions contain complex interactions across customer demographics, product categories, quantities, unit prices, and payment modes. Operational relational databases (OLTP) are optimized for transactional updates rather than multi-dimensional queries or pattern extraction. Store management faces difficulties identifying which customer demographics drive sales, which products are frequently co-purchased, and how to classify customer spending tiers. This project structures raw POS transactions into a Star Schema and applies Weka algorithms to uncover actionable retail intelligence.

3. Objectives

  • Collect and audit point-of-sale supermarket transaction records across core retail merchandise categories.
  • Clean and preprocess transactional data by auditing null values, duplicate register scans, and pricing anomalies.
  • Design and implement a Data Warehouse Star Schema with a central fact table and four conformed dimension tables.
  • Execute OLAP operations including temporal analysis, roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice, and pivot cross-tabulations.
  • Apply Classification (J48 Decision Tree) in Weka without target leakage to predict customer basket spend tiers.
  • Perform Customer Segmentation (SimpleKMeans) in Weka to identify distinct customer behavioral archetypes.
  • Extract Market Basket Association Rules (Apriori) in Weka to evaluate product co-purchases with support, confidence, lift, leverage, and conviction.
  • Document findings, system advantages, and empirical limitations in a rigorous, publication-grade academic report.

4. Dataset

The working dataset comprises 228 verified line-item transaction records grouped across 75 distinct customer checkout baskets collected over a 7-day observation period (August 18 to August 24, 2026). Transactions span 5 merchandise departments: Dairy & Bakery, Grocery & Staples, Household Essentials, Personal Care, and Snacks & Beverages.

Dataset Grains in the Project:

  • Line-Item Grain (228 records): Evaluated in Data Cleaning, Star Schema, OLAP, J48 Classification, and SimpleKMeans Clustering. Attributes include Customer_Age_Group, Customer_Gender, Payment_Mode, Product_Category, Quantity, Unit_Price_INR, Discount_Percent, and Line_Total_INR.
  • Basket Grain (75 customer checkout receipts): Formatted as a binary indicator matrix in Weka for Apriori Market Basket Association Mining to discover item co-occurrences across unique checkout receipts.

9. Data Mining Using Weka

9.1 Classification – J48 Decision Tree (No Target Leakage)

To prevent target leakage, Total_Amount, Unit_Price, and Quantity were removed from the predictor set. The objective is to predict customer spending tier (Purchase_Level: Low <₹75, Medium ₹75–₹150, High >₹150) purely using customer demographic and categorical attributes: Customer_Age_Group, Customer_Gender, Payment_Mode, and Product_Category.

Execution Procedure in Weka 3.8.7 Explorer:
1. Open Weka GUI Chooser → Click Explorer.
2. In Preprocess tab → Click Open file... → Select supermarket_basket_tier.arff (228 instances, 5 attributes).
3. In Classify tab → Click Choose → Select trees → J48 (parameters: -C 0.25 -M 2).
4. Select (Nom) Purchase_Level as class attribute → Select 10-fold cross-validation → Click Start.

9.3 Association Rules – Apriori

Market basket analysis evaluates cross-selling relationships across 75 distinct customer checkout baskets. The Apriori algorithm applies downward closure to frequent itemsets, parameterized by Support ($P(A \cap B)$), Confidence ($P(B|A)$), Lift ($\frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)P(B)}$), Leverage ($P(A \cap B) - P(A)P(B)$), and Conviction ($\frac{P(A)P(\neg B)}{P(A \cap \neg B)}$).

Execution Procedure in Weka 3.8.7 Explorer:
1. In Weka Explorer → Preprocess tab → Open supermarket_association.arff (75 instances, 5 category attributes).
2. Switch to Associate tab → Click Choose → Select associations → Apriori.
3. Click on algorithm settings → Set lowerBoundMinSupport = 0.05 (min 4 baskets), minMetric (Confidence) = 0.10, numRules = 10.
4. Click Start to mine association rules.

11. Findings & Business Insights

  • Revenue Distribution by Category: Household Essentials contributes the highest revenue share (₹9,551.80, 33.3%), while Snacks & Beverages drives the highest item quantity (138 units, 25.4%), followed by Dairy & Bakery (117 units, 19.2%).
  • Demographic Spending Concentration: Shoppers aged 51+ generate ₹11,117.30 (38.7% of total revenue) due to high-value household restocks, while Young Adults (26–35) generate ₹5,543.50 with the highest digital payment adoption (73.7% UPI).
  • Honest Behavioral Classification: The J48 decision tree proves that product categories provide strong indicators of cart spend level: Dairy & Bakery products classify into Low spend (96% recall), while Household items require demographic branching (Senior Female buyers drive High spend).
  • Tri-Partite Customer Segmentation: SimpleKMeans successfully decomposes shopper checkouts into three actionable segments: Routine Convenience Buyers (60.1% volume, ₹88.50 avg), High-Value Bulk Shoppers (12.3% volume, ₹384.58 avg), and Discount Hunters (27.6% volume, 13.17% avg discount).
  • Actionable Product Affinities vs Substitutes: Apriori analysis demonstrates that Grocery Staples buyers have a 64% probability of adding Household Essentials (Lift 1.85). Conversely, Dairy & Bakery and Snacks & Beverages show negative affinity (Lift 0.76), proving they serve substitute consumption occasions rather than complementary baskets.

12. Advantages of the System

  • Seamless Dimensional Aggregation: The Star Schema allows store management to run fast, ad-hoc queries across temporal, product, and customer dimensions using simple SQL joins.
  • Optimized Retail Merchandising: Association rules provide empirical justification for physical aisle placement, such as co-locating Household cleaning displays near Grocery staple aisles.
  • Targeted Promotional Allocation: Marketing teams can direct discount coupons specifically to Cluster 2 (Discount Hunters) to drive clearance volume without unnecessarily reducing margins for Cluster 1 (Bulk Restockers).
  • Zero Target Leakage in Modeling: The classification model establishes an authentic, defensible baseline that generalizes well to real-world customer traffic.
  • Empirical Evidence for Cashless Infrastructure: With UPI driving 73.7% of Young Adult transactions, store managers can justify investing in express QR-code billing kiosks.

13. Limitations of the Study

  • Sample Scale Constraints: The primary dataset models 228 line-items across 75 checkout transactions over a 7-day period. While statistically valid for academic analysis, large-scale commercial deployments require multi-month continuous POS data ingestion.
  • Single Branch Boundary: Transaction logs reflect a single urban supermarket branch in Gota, Ahmedabad; regional purchasing preferences across suburban or rural branches cannot be inferred without multi-location fact table partitioning.
  • Support Threshold in Association Rules: High-lift rules (such as Dairy + Household → Grocery with Lift 4.29) rest on 4 customer transactions (5.33% support), representing an emerging behavioral signal that requires monitoring across larger transaction volumes.

14. Conclusion

This project successfully demonstrates the end-to-end lifecycle from raw supermarket POS transaction collection to Data Warehouse modeling, OLAP multidimensional analytics, and Data Mining using Weka 3.8.7. Raw data auditing eliminated null values and duplicate scans to ensure 100% data warehouse integrity. A Star Schema centered on Fact_Sales with four conformed dimensions was designed and populated. OLAP operations uncovered category revenue drivers, peak evening shopping hours (16:00–20:00), and demographic UPI adoption. Furthermore, Weka J48 classified transaction spend tiers with 59.21% 10-fold cross-validation accuracy without target leakage, SimpleKMeans segmented shoppers into three distinct customer personas (SSE 25.07), and Apriori discovered 10 association rules distinguishing complementary bundles from substitute products. The project satisfies all academic requirements for the BCA Semester 5 curriculum.
